The medications

  • Estradiol (Estrace)

    Pill at bedtime, Oct 2 until injections start

    A form of estrogen. It keeps the follicles at a similar size so they grow together once injections begin, which helps more eggs mature at the same time.

  • Gonal-f

    Evening injection, about 10 to 12 days

    Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). It tells the ovaries to grow many follicles at once instead of the usual one.

  • Menopur

    Evening injection, same days as Gonal-f

    A mix of FSH and LH. It works alongside Gonal-f to help the follicles and the eggs inside them develop well.

  • Cetrotide

    Morning injection, starting around day 5 of injections

    The brakes. It stops the body from releasing the eggs on its own before the clinic can retrieve them.

  • Novarel and Lupron (the trigger)

    Once, at an exact time set by the clinic

    Together they give the eggs the final push to mature and get ready for release. Retrieval happens about 36 hours later, which is why the timing matters so much.

  • Folic acid

    Daily throughout

    A B vitamin the clinic recommends taking during treatment to support egg and overall health.

Retrieval day

  • She needs a ride home. The clinic requires someone to drive her after sedation. No rideshare alone.
  • Plan a quiet rest day. Couch, heating pad, easy food, plenty of water.
  • Cramping and bloating are expected. Tylenol helps. Ibuprofen is OK again after retrieval unless the clinic says otherwise.
  • The exact date comes late. The clinic picks it based on how she responds, often with only a couple of days' notice.

Good to know

During treatment Isa can't take ibuprofen, only Tylenol, and should skip high-impact or contact activities.

If something feels urgent, like severe pain, trouble breathing, or very fast swelling of the belly, call the UCSF clinic's 24/7 line: 415-353-7475.

Know the parts

The pen
  • Pen cap: the white cap on the needle end
  • Reservoir: the clear section that holds the medicine
  • Dose display: the small window that shows a number
  • Dose knob: the red end you turn and press
The needle (a new one every time)
  • Peel-off seal: the paper tab on top
  • Outer needle cap: the big plastic cap. Keep it.
  • Inner shield: the thin green cover. Throw it away.
Checklist

  1. 0Set up

    Don't warm the pen in a microwave or with any other heat.

  2. 1Get ready

    Don't shake the pen, since that makes air bubbles. Don't use scissors or a knife on the box, and don't use a pen that's expired, cracked, or was dropped.

  3. 2Choose and clean the spot

    Don't touch or blow on the spot after cleaning it.

  4. 3Attach the needle

    Don't put the thin inner shield back on the needle. That's how needle sticks happen.

  5. !First time using this pen?

    • New pen: look for a drop of liquid at the needle tip. If you see one, go to step 4. If you don't, or you aren't sure, do "Make a droplet" below first.
    • Pen you've used before: skip this and go to step 4.

  6. 4Dial your dose

    Don't push or pull the knob while turning it. If you go past your dose, just turn it back. If you're not sure of today's dose, call the clinic.

  7. 5Inject

    Don't let go of the knob while the needle is still in. Going in at an angle can bend the needle.

    • Shows 0: you got the full dose.
    • Shows another number: that's how much is still missing. Write it in the diary, then do steps 1 to 7 again with a new pen, dialing that number.

  8. 6Remove the needle

  9. 7Write it down

  10. +Make a droplet (new pen only)

    No droplet? Call the clinic before injecting.
